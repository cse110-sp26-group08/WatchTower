## Discussion

### What The Group Did Well

The thread responses consistently highlighted strong execution, good morale, and effective team structure.

Common strengths mentioned included:

- Splitting into subteams quickly and effectively
- Completing assigned tasks on time
- Giving teammates clear direction on what needed to be produced
- Maintaining strong morale and willingness to contribute
- Using standups to keep everyone informed
- Reaching out to the professor and TA when the team needed clarity
- Taking research and early project planning seriously rather than treating tasks as checkboxes

Several responses emphasized that the team was productive early because roles were established quickly and contributors started working as soon as responsibilities were clear.

### What Specific Teams Did Well

The responses described strong performance from both frontend and backend-oriented groups, along with positive feedback for the research and UX/UI efforts.

Frontend strengths mentioned in the thread included:

- Splitting work well across teammates
- Executing assigned tasks reliably
- Bringing different perspectives into design and implementation work
- Supporting and encouraging each other

Backend strengths mentioned in the thread included:

- Strong communication
- Good collaboration around data collection and storage design
- Detailed thinking around endpoints and database planning
- Clear coordination when clarifying confusing project requirements

Other team-level strengths mentioned included:

- The UX/UI team meeting with the professor and TA to gain clearer project direction
- Research groups producing helpful information that shaped the overall plan
- Small teams coordinating well when project requirements were ambiguous

Overall, the responses suggest that subteams functioned well internally and often helped improve direction for the entire project.

### What Should Improve Next Sprint

The most common improvement themes were clarity, communication, and task definition.

Requested improvements included:

- Defining future tasks more clearly before the sprint starts
- Prioritizing features more explicitly
- Distinguishing core features from nice-to-haves and rabbit holes
- Improving cross-team communication so everyone stays aligned
- Communicating faster when questions or blockers arise
- Assigning more specific tasks within each subteam
- Rotating roles more intentionally to reduce the bus factor
- Handling standup and retrospective task assignment earlier

Across the thread, the main concern was not effort or motivation, but making the next sprint more structured so development can move faster with less confusion.

## Takeaways and Summary

This retrospective suggests Sprint 1 established a strong foundation for the team.

1. **The group worked well in parallel:** subteams formed quickly and contributors generally completed work on time.
2. **Team morale and effort were strong:** multiple responses noted that people were engaged and willing to help.
3. **Subteams added real value:** frontend, backend, UX/UI, and research efforts all contributed useful direction and planning.
4. **Escalating questions early helped:** meeting with the professor and TA was repeatedly mentioned as an important success.
5. **The next step is structure:** the biggest need for Sprint 2 is clearer prioritization, tighter task assignment, and stronger cross-team alignment.

**Key follow-up items:**

- Define sprint goals and task ownership more clearly up front
- Improve communication between subteams during the sprint
- Prioritize core features before optional work
- Break team goals into more specific assignments inside each subteam
- Share knowledge more broadly so fewer tasks depend on one person
